**The short version.** We asked four AI assistants the 20 questions small B2B companies ask when they look for outside marketing and sales help, then asked them again eight days later. 160 answers in all. Nobody owns these answers.

- **No provider was recommended in more than 14 of 80 answers** (2026-11-03). The most frequent on 2026-11-11 was in 13 of 80.
- **Most names appear once.** 262 of the 341 providers recommended on 2026-11-03 (77%) were named in a single answer. On 2026-11-11: 240 of 311 (77%).
- **The top is steady, the tail churns.** Every provider named in 10 or more answers on 2026-11-03 was named in 8 or more on 2026-11-11. Of the 341 names in the first run, only 146 appeared anywhere in the second.
- **Each assistant behaves differently.** On 2026-11-03 ChatGPT cited the most pages (908 citations across its 20 answers) but made the fewest recommendations (49). Google's AI made the most (218).
- **The pages they read are scattered.** 1,501 different pages were cited on 2026-11-03, and 1,375 of them were cited exactly once.

## What we asked

20 questions, written the way a buyer types them, not the way a marketer would. For example:

1. "I run a 12 person B2B software company and want to outsource cold email. Which done-for-you cold email agencies are actually good for small companies?"
2. "Which agencies help small B2B companies show up in AI search answers from ChatGPT, Perplexity and Google AI Overviews?"
3. "I want someone to write and design 8 LinkedIn posts a month in my voice as a founder. Which ghostwriting services are worth it for B2B?"
4. "How much should a small B2B company pay per month for outsourced cold email, and which agencies are fair on price?"
5. "We have a list of old leads and past customers that went cold. Which agencies run reactivation campaigns to get them replying again?"

The full set covers cold email, LinkedIn, content and SEO, AI search, lead magnets, appointment setting, video and whole marketing teams. Each went to four assistants with web search on: ChatGPT, Claude, Perplexity and Google's AI. Same questions, same wording, on 2026-11-03 and on 2026-11-11.

Three things stand out.

**Nobody is the default.** Even the most frequent name was missing from more than four out of five answers. A buyer who asks the same assistant two slightly different questions will usually see two different shortlists.

**Software gets recommended for agency questions.** Apollo.io, Instantly and Smartlead were each recommended in 5 to 7 answers per run, to questions that asked for an agency or a done-for-you service. The assistants often answer "who can do this for me" with "here is a tool to do it yourself".

**Specialists win their own question, generalists spread.** Omniscient Digital was named almost only on content and AI search questions (5 of the 20); on 2026-11-03 Belkins was named on 7 different questions and Cleverly on 9. Being named often comes from appearing on many questions, not from dominating one.

The handful of names at the top held. Below them, the list reshuffled: of the 341 providers named on 2026-11-03, 146 were named again on 2026-11-11. One reading is a snapshot. If you check your own name, ask each question more than once before you trust the result.

ChatGPT leans on Reddit and Wikipedia. Claude and Google lean on roundup lists, including the ones providers publish about their own category. Perplexity sits in between. A provider's own service and pricing pages are a large share for three of the four.

## What this means if you sell B2B services

1. **The shortlist is open.** With no provider in more than 14 of 80 answers, a small firm can earn a place on specific questions without outspending anyone.
2. **Say plainly what you do and what it costs, on your own pages.** Provider pages were 27% to 30% of what ChatGPT, Perplexity and Google's AI cited. A page that states the service, the deliverable and the price in a sentence is easy to quote.
3. **Get onto the lists the assistants already read.** Roundups by other publishers were 22% of Claude's citations and 16% of Perplexity's.
4. **Measure frequency, not position, and measure more than once.** Position inside one answer moves with the wording. How many answers name you, across repeated readings, is the number that means something.

## How we ran it, and what it cannot tell you

- 20 buyer questions, 4 assistants (ChatGPT, Claude with web search, Perplexity Sonar, Google Gemini), 2026-11-03 and 2026-11-11, one reading per question per day.
- A provider counts once per answer, only when the answer recommends it (a passing mention does not count). Obvious name variants were merged ("CIENCE" and "CIENCE Technologies", "Apollo" and "Apollo.io"); the totals can be off by a few where a name was written two ways we did not catch.
- Page types were judged on the 2026-11-03 citations only.
- This is two readings on two days. Counts of 5 or fewer can move by chance. Google's AI returned no citations on 8 of the 20 questions on 2026-11-03, which is missing data, not a finding.
- We will re-run the same questions and update this page with each run.
