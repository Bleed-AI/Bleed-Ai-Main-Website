<li>Have <strong>identifiable client outcomes</strong> — real case studies, not just testimonials with first names redacted</li>

2f:Tbd7,<h2 id="the-state-of-cold-email-in-2026">The state of cold email in 2026</h2>
<p>A snapshot of what's actually happening in inboxes this year (with sources):</p>
<ul>
<li><strong>Platform-wide average reply rate: 3.43%</strong> — across 1.6M+ accounts on Instantly in 2026 (<a href="https://instantly.ai">source</a>). Down from ~5% in 2023.</li>
<li><strong>Top quartile: 5.5%+</strong> | <strong>Top 10%: 10.7%+</strong> — the gap between average and elite has widened, which means the meta has shifted from "send more" to "send smarter."</li>
<li><strong>Industry split:</strong> Legal Services replies at ~10% (highest), SaaS/Software at 1.9–3.5% (lowest). If you're in SaaS expecting 10% reply rates, the math ain't mathing.</li>
<li><strong>5% of cold email senders personalize every email.</strong> The other 95% are doing <code>{{first_name}}</code> and calling it strategy.</li>
<li><strong>58% of replies come from the first email; 42% from follow-ups.</strong> Yet <strong>48% of senders never send a follow-up at all.</strong> Free money on the table.</li>
<li><strong>67% of B2B sending domains have at least one critical authentication error</strong> — per MailDeck's 2026 audit of 1,000+ domains. Most of those got there from running cold outbound on a primary domain that wasn't set up for it.</li>
<li><strong>The "100 emails/day per inbox" rule is dead.</strong> Real warming data shows 30–50/day is the safer ceiling for established domains in 2026. Anyone telling you to send 300/day from a single mailbox wants to burn your domain and move on to the next client.</li>
</ul>

31:Tcb9,<h2 id="what-separates-a-good-cold-email-agency-from-a-bad-one">What separates a good cold email agency from a bad one</h2>
<p>There are five things that matter. Most agencies are dishonest about at least two of them.</p>
<div class="not-prose" style="margin:1.5rem 0;padding:1.25rem 1.5rem;border-radius:14px;border:1px solid rgba(239,68,68,0.35);background:rgba(239,68,68,0.06)">
  <div style="display:flex;align-items:center;gap:0.5rem;margin-bottom:0.5rem;font-size:0.7rem;font-weight:700;text-transform:uppercase;letter-spacing:0.06em;color:#ef4444">
    <span style="font-size:1rem">⚠️</span> The Critical Question
  </div>
  <p style="margin:0 0 0.5rem 0;color:var(--text-primary);font-size:1.1rem;font-weight:600;line-height:1.5"><em>"Whose domain are you sending from?"</em></p>
  <p style="margin:0;color:var(--text-secondary);line-height:1.65">If the agency can't give you a straight answer in 10 seconds — or wants to send from <strong style="color:var(--text-primary)">your primary domain</strong> — walk away. One bad campaign permanently damages your business email deliverability.</p>
</div>
<p><strong>1. Whose domain are you sending from?</strong> 🚩
The single most important question. Reputable agencies own and warm their own secondary domains (e.g. <code>getyourcompany.com</code>, <code>yourcompany.io</code>) and send from those. If anyone wants to send from your primary domain (<code>yourcompany.com</code>), walk away. One bad campaign permanently damages deliverability.</p>
<p><strong>2. Reply rate vs. positive reply rate.</strong>
"15% reply rate" sounds great until you find out 90% of replies are "unsubscribe" or "stop emailing me." Always ask: <em>what's the positive reply rate?</em> If they don't track it, that's a tell.</p>
<p><strong>3. Targeting layer.</strong>
The agencies that win in 2026 spend more time on signals (hiring intent, funding events, tech-stack changes, executive moves) than on email copy. If an agency leads with their "AI-powered email copywriting," they're optimizing the wrong layer.</p>
<p><strong>4. Pilot availability.</strong>
The good ones are confident enough to offer a small paid (or sometimes free) pilot. The mediocre ones lock you into a 6–12 month minimum. Confidence shows up in contract length.</p>
<p><strong>5. Transparent reporting.</strong>
Look for: positive reply rate, qualified meetings booked, pipeline generated, opportunities-to-meeting ratio. Not just opens (Apple Mail Privacy Protection killed open rates as a useful metric in 2021).</p>
<div class="not-prose" style="margin:1.5rem 0;padding:0.95rem 1.25rem;border-radius:12px;border-left:3px solid #3b82f6;background:rgba(59,130,246,0.05)">
  <div style="font-size:0.7rem;font-weight:700;text-transform:uppercase;letter-spacing:0.06em;color:#3b82f6;margin-bottom:0.35rem">💡 Good to know</div>
  <p style="margin:0;color:var(--text-secondary);line-height:1.6;font-size:0.95rem">Most agencies fail criteria <strong style="color:var(--text-primary)">#2 and #4</strong>. They quote reply rate without positive reply rate, and they require 6–12 month commitments with no pilot. If you find an agency that gets #1, #2, and #4 right, you've already eliminated ~80% of the market.</p>

<p>Five-step decision filter, in order:</p>
<ol>
<li><strong>Disqualify on infrastructure first.</strong> Anyone wanting to send from your primary domain is out. Anyone using shared SMTP pools is out. <em>Don't negotiate on this.</em></li>
<li><strong>Match the price tier to your LTV.</strong> If your LTV is $5K, a $10K/mo agency is mathematically going to lose you money for 6+ months even if they perform. The math has to math.</li>
<li><strong>Test the contract length.</strong> Ask for a 30-day or single-pilot option. The answer reveals the agency's confidence in their process. <em>Confidence shows up in contract length.</em></li>
<li><strong>Read one real case study.</strong> Not the homepage testimonials. Ask for a case study with: industry, company size, campaign duration, sequences sent, reply rate, <strong>positive</strong> reply rate, qualified meetings booked, pipeline generated. <li><strong>Pilot, then scale.</strong> No matter who you pick, start small. A pilot is the cheapest way to learn that the offer/ICP/messaging combination doesn't work yet.</li>
