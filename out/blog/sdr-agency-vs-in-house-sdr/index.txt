31:T4ee,<h2 id="the-60-second-answer">The 60-second answer</h2>
<p>Pick <strong>an in-house SDR team</strong> if:</p>
<ul>
<li>You've raised $10M+ and have a VP of Sales who has scaled an SDR org before</li>
<li>Your sales cycle is highly technical or product-led — only insiders can qualify meetings correctly</li>
<li>Your offer + ICP is stable and you need repeatable volume from institutional knowledge</li>
<li>You can absorb 3–6 months of ramp time before the first meeting</li>
</ul>
<p>Pick <strong>an SDR agency</strong> if:</p>
<ul>
<li>You're seed to Series A, or bootstrapped, and every month of runway matters</li>
<li>You're validating a new ICP, offer, or geography and don't want to hire on speculation</li>
<li>You need meetings booked in 1–2 weeks, not 3–6 months</li>
<li>Your sales leadership doesn't have SDR-management experience</li>
</ul>
<p>Do <strong>both</strong> if:</p>
<ul>
<li>You're at $10M+ ARR with a mature sales org</li>
<li>You want in-house owning strategic accounts and complex qualification</li>
<li>You want an agency owning top-of-funnel volume and new-segment testing</li>
<li>You want to hedge against SDR churn (agencies don't quit at 3 AM)</li>
</ul>
<p>The rest of this article is the "why" behind those calls.</p>

<p><strong>Pros:</strong></p>
<ul>
<li><strong>Meetings in 1–2 weeks.</strong> No 45-day hiring cycle, no 3-month ramp.</li>
<li><strong>Owns infrastructure.</strong> Reputable agencies (BleedAI, Belkins, Martal, CIENCE) send from their own pre-warmed secondary domains — your primary domain reputation stays clean.</li>
<li><strong>De-risks experimentation.</strong> Want to test a new segment? Agency runs a pilot campaign for $683–$5K. Same experiment in-house means hiring an SDR you might not need in 6 months.</li>
<li><strong>No HR overhead.</strong> Sick days, PTO, benefits, comp reviews, terminations — not your problem.</li>
<li><strong>Learning across accounts.</strong> Good agencies see 30+ campaigns per year across industries; that pattern-matching is hard to replicate in-house.</li>
</ul>
<p><strong>Cons:</strong></p>
<ul>
<li><strong>You don't own the institutional knowledge.</strong> When the contract ends, so does the accumulated learning about your ICP.</li>
<li><strong>Quality varies wildly.</strong> A bad agency can be worse than no agency (burned domains, spammed prospects, damaged brand). Due diligence matters.</li>
<li><strong>Contract lock-ins.</strong> Many agencies require 6–12 month minimums. Confidence shows up in contract length — the good ones offer pilots.</li>
<li><strong>Culture disconnect.</strong> An agency rep will never care about your company the way an employee does. This matters more for complex enterprise sales than for volume outreach.</li>
</ul>

38:T638,<h2 id="where-the-hybrid-model-actually-shines">Where the "hybrid" model actually shines</h2>
<p>The reality most sales leaders don't publicly discuss: <strong>at $10M+ ARR, virtually every successful B2B outbound org runs both models simultaneously.</strong></p>
<p>Structure that works most often:</p>
<ul>
<li><strong>1–2 senior in-house SDRs</strong> — assigned to top-tier target accounts (Fortune 5000, strategic partnerships, competitive displacement plays). These accounts are too valuable to hand to an agency. In-house reps handle the multi-touch, multi-stakeholder qualification that outsiders can't.</li>
<li><strong>Agency for volume</strong> — everything below the top 100 target accounts. Agency runs signal-based campaigns across mid-market and SMB segments, feeding qualified meetings back to a dedicated in-house AE.</li>
<li><strong>In-house SDR = 20% of accounts, 60% of pipeline value.</strong> Agency = 80% of accounts, 40% of pipeline value. Both matter.</li>
</ul>
<div class="not-prose" style="margin:1.5rem 0;padding:0.95rem 1.25rem;border-radius:12px;border-left:3px solid #3b82f6;background:rgba(59,130,246,0.05)">
  <div style="font-size:0.7rem;font-weight:700;text-transform:uppercase;letter-spacing:0.06em;color:#3b82f6;margin-bottom:0.35rem">💡 Good to know</div>
  <p style="margin:0;color:var(--text-secondary);line-height:1.6;font-size:0.95rem">The hybrid model is roughly 30% more expensive per month than pure in-house — but produces 2–3x the pipeline. If your unit economics allow, it's almost always the right answer at scale.</p>

<p>Five filters in order:</p>
<ol>
<li><strong>Timeline gate.</strong> If you can't afford to wait 4–6 months for the first meeting, in-house is off the table. Pick an agency.</li>
<li><strong>Sales leadership gate.</strong> If you don't have someone who has managed an SDR team before, agency (or hire the VP first). SDR hires without experienced management fail 70% of the time.</li>
<li><strong>Product complexity gate.</strong> If your product requires deep technical qualification, in-house wins. If it's a repeatable playbook, agency wins.</li>
<li><strong>Unit economics gate.</strong> Does your LTV support $150K/rep/year? Under $5K LTV, in-house math gets hard. Agency scales down more gracefully.</li>
<li><strong>Strategic value gate.</strong> Are these accounts you MUST own institutionally (competitors, partners, strategic clients)? Those go in-house. Everything else can go to an agency.</li>
</ol>

3d:T87d,<h2 id="final-thoughts">Final thoughts</h2>
<p>The wrong question is "agency or in-house — which is better?" The right question is <strong>"what does my current stage require, and what will my next stage require?"</strong></p>
<p>Most teams answer "in-house" because it feels more legitimate ("we're serious about sales"). Most teams should answer "agency" because they're pre-Series-B, don't have a VP of Sales, and can't afford six months of quiet before the first meeting.</p>
<p>Then, when the agency has proved outbound works for your offer, you graduate — build 1–2 senior in-house SDRs for strategic accounts, keep the agency running for volume, and become one of the many $10M+ B2B teams that discovered the hybrid model is quietly the most efficient version of this whole system.</p>
<div class="not-prose" style="margin:1.5rem 0;padding:1.5rem;border-radius:14px;border:1px solid rgba(177,19,15,0.35);background:linear-gradient(135deg,rgba(177,19,15,0.08),rgba(177,19,15,0.02))">

  <p style="margin:0;color:var(--text-primary);font-size:1.05rem;line-height:1.55"><strong>2.</strong> <em>"Can we afford 4–6 months of ramp before the first qualified meeting?"</em></p>
